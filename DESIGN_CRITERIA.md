## HOW TO SCORE
Each category has 4 criteria scored 0–2.
0 = fails  |  1 = partially meets  |  2 = fully meets
Max per slide: 20. Minimum to pass: 18.

---

## NARRATIVE CRITERIA — Does the story land? (max 8)

- [ ] The slide serves exactly one idea. State it in 6 words or fewer. If you can't, it has two ideas.
- [ ] The slide fits its position in the arc: Why (S2–S4) → Who (S5–S7) → Culture (S8–S11) → The Plan (S12–S16) → Close (S17). A viewer moving forward should feel pulled, not confused.
- [ ] Section openers (S2, S5, S8, S12) work as standalones. If a viewer only saw this slide, would they want the next one?
- [ ] The close (S17) lands harder than the opener (S1). Stats support a feeling — not a resume.

---

## VISUAL CRITERIA — Does it feel like Cursor made this? (max 8)

- [ ] Dark background (#080808) is the only background. No slide drifts lighter.
- [ ] One visual accent per slide maximum. Green (#4ade80) is reserved for active/live items only — not decoration.
- [ ] Text hierarchy is legible at a glance: one H-level dominates, body supports, mono labels recede.
- [ ] Visual breathing room exists. Content does not crowd the edges or feel compressed.

---

## DENSITY CRITERIA — Is it a presentation or a document? (max 8)

- [ ] No slide has more than 6 bullet-style items. If it does, collapse or cut.
- [ ] No paragraph of body text exceeds 25 words. Anything longer belongs in the spoken script, not on screen.
- [ ] Every signal block earns its place. It adds a dimension not visible in the main content — it does not repeat it.
- [ ] JetBrains Mono labels are sparse. No more than 4 visible per slide.

---

## AUTHENTICITY CRITERIA — Does it sound like John? (max 8)

- [ ] First-person voice is present in at least one element — signal, quote, or brow item.
- [ ] No slide could have been made by a candidate who hadn't lived the Salesforce×Cursor rollout.
- [ ] Specific proof points (26→4 days, 90%+ adoption, Coach of the Year, Value Cards app) appear where they're most powerful — not clustered only on the close slide.
- [ ] Diggory and Sahil are addressed directly at least once each across the deck, and placement feels natural not forced.

---

## PACING CRITERIA — Does it work in a 30-minute room? (max 8 — shared across deck, prorated per slide)

- [ ] S1–S4 (the Why arc) can be delivered in under 4 minutes combined. No single slide in this arc feels like it needs more than 90 seconds.
- [ ] S12 (arc overview) is a genuine pause — lighter in density than S13–S15. It's a breath, not a slide.
- [ ] S17 (close) requires zero explanation. It should be felt, not read.
- [ ] Any slide with a two-column layout genuinely uses both columns at equal weight. If one column is thin, collapse it.

---

## AESTHETIC + VISUAL COMMUNICATION CRITERIA — Are ideas represented in the strongest form? (max 8)

- [ ] Representation fit: each slide uses the best form for its message (statement, comparison, process, proof, quote, or metric) instead of defaulting to bullets.
- [ ] Scannability in 3 seconds: the headline + dominant visual element communicate the takeaway before body text is read.
- [ ] Visual rhythm across the deck: consecutive slides do not repeat the same composition pattern more than twice unless intentionally used for emphasis.
- [ ] Alignment discipline: consistent vertical anchors, column starts, spacing cadence, and edge margins; no accidental drift between slides.

---

## EXECUTIVE SLIDECRAFT CHECKS (for 19/20+ target quality)

- [ ] One-message test: if the presenter goes silent for 5 seconds, the audience can still infer the point.
- [ ] Contrast-of-importance: important ideas have meaningfully stronger visual weight than supporting details.
- [ ] Signal economy: signal blocks are used only when they add a new dimension; remove if they restate bullets.
- [ ] Proof distribution: high-impact proof points are dispersed through the arc, not stacked at the close.
- [ ] Pause architecture: section openers and transition slides are intentionally lighter than surrounding execution slides.
